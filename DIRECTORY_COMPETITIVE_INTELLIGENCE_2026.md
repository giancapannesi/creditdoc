### 1.1 NerdWallet — $837M Revenue (2025), IPO at $5.2B

**How they started:**
Tim Chen was a former hedge fund analyst. In August 2009, his sister asked which credit card to get. He Googled it, found only "infomercials and not helpful" results, built a spreadsheet comparing cards from the biggest banks, then turned it into a website. Initial investment: $800. First product: a web app comparing credit cards. Official launch: March 2010.

**Revenue model:**
- Affiliate commissions from credit cards, loans, insurance, banking products (primary)
- Display advertising (secondary)
- Lead generation for financial products
- 2025 breakdown: Lending revenue +141% YoY (personal loans +264%), Insurance strong, Credit Cards -24% (organic search decline)

**Time to first revenue:**
- Year 1 (2009): $75 total revenue
- Year 2 (2010): $65,000 — "a pretty decent audience"
- Years 3-6: Bootstrapped, no outside funding, profitable
- Year 7 (2015): $64M Series A — by this point already profitable with 10M users
- Year 12 (2021): IPO at $5.2B, $380M revenue

**SEO strategy — the specific mechanics:**
1. **Topic clusters:** Built interconnected content clusters around every financial product category. The mortgage pillar page alone drives 4.4M organic sessions/month.
2. **Content velocity:** Set a goal of 500 pieces of high-quality content per month. First hire was a content writer, not an engineer.
3. **Awards badge link building:** Created "Best Places for Young Families" awards for 10 towns in every state. Winners put NerdWallet badges on their homepages, generating hundreds of high-DA homepage backlinks. This is one of NerdWallet's most brilliant SEO tactics — "ego bait" at scale.
4. **Siege Media partnership:** Hired Siege Media for SEO content strategy, which grew organic traffic by 800% over four years through data-driven, link-worthy content.
5. **Calculator pages as link magnets:** Mortgage calculator = 172 referring domains. Home affordability calculator = 585 referring domains. These tools compound backlinks over years.

**Key inflection point:**
2012 was a near-death moment — revenue flattened, Chen considered returning to Wall Street. He decided to double down on content instead. The decision to hire writers (not engineers) and treat content as the core product was the turning point.

**Data moat:**
- Proprietary household debt surveys (earned 2,600+ referring domains)
- 100+ person editorial team including Pulitzer Prize winners
- Brand recognition (73% organic/direct traffic by 2021)
- 1.3M keywords ranked, 5.7M monthly organic visits

**Current traffic:** ~18M monthly organic visitors (though declining due to AI Overviews)

**Critical 2025-2026 warning:** NerdWallet's Q4 2025 earnings explicitly stated "strength in performance marketing, direct, and non-search referral channels more than offset organic search headwinds as consumers turned to AI overviews and LLMs over traditional search." Credit card revenue dropped 24% YoY due to organic search decline. This is a structural warning for anyone building a pure-SEO financial directory.

**What CreditDoc can learn:**
- The awards/badge strategy is directly replicable: "Best Credit Repair Companies in [State]" awards with embeddable badges
- Content velocity matters more than perfection — 500 pieces/month was their target
- Bootstrapping for 6 years forced capital discipline that made the business sustainable
- Tools (calculators, quizzes) are permanent link magnets that compound over years
- Build brand and direct traffic NOW — organic search is declining in finance

### 1.2 Credit Karma — $2.3B Revenue (2025 under Intuit), Acquired for $8.1B

**How they started:**
Founded 2007 by Ken Lin, Ryan Graciano, and Nichole Mustard. Lin's insight: consumers paid $15-20/month for credit monitoring. What if it were free? Launched February 2008. Got $500K angel round from Pathfinder VC, then $2.5M Series A from QED Investors in 2009. Then went 3.5 years without raising again — proof of capital efficiency.

**Revenue model — how they monetize without charging users:**
1. **Referral commissions (primary):** When a user applies for and is approved for a credit card, loan, or insurance product recommended on the platform, the financial institution pays Credit Karma a commission. This is sophisticated affiliate marketing powered by personalized matching.
2. **Approval Odds engine:** Analyzes thousands of data points to predict likelihood of approval. Runs 60B+ daily AI predictions. Compares your credit profile to other members who were approved. This matching drives higher conversion rates (= higher payouts from partners).
3. **Interchange fees:** Revenue from their debit card products (Visa interchange 1.4-2.5%).
4. **Interest income:** Uses cash on users' accounts to lend to other institutions, collects interest.
5. **Tax filing:** Intuit integrated TurboTax-adjacent tax services into Credit Karma.

**Time to first revenue:**
- 2007-2008: Building, no revenue
- 2009: First real revenue from credit card affiliate commissions
- 2009-2013: Bootstrapped growth on ~$5M total capital (Lin stated they were "never more than net negative about $5M")
- 2019: ~$1B revenue (unaudited)
- 2020: Acquired by Intuit for $8.1B ($3.4B cash + $4.7B Intuit stock)
- 2025: Credit Karma revenue $2.3B under Intuit (grew 32%)

**SEO strategy:**
- Free credit scores were the SEO hook — "free credit score" is a massive search query
- Every user who checked their score created a return visit habit
- Educational content around credit topics built topical authority
- 110M+ members created network effects in data quality

**Key inflection point:**
The "give away what others charge for" decision. Before Credit Karma, credit scores cost money. Making them free was the single decision that enabled everything else — massive user base, personalized recommendations, and the data flywheel.

**Data moat:**
- 110M+ member profiles with full credit histories
- Behavioral data: what products people apply for, what they get approved for, at what credit scores
- This data trains their matching algorithm, making recommendations more accurate, driving higher conversion rates, earning more revenue
- Network effect: more users = better data = better recommendations = more users

**Current traffic:** 37M MAU at time of Intuit acquisition; now integrated into Intuit ecosystem

**What CreditDoc can learn:**
- The "free tool as hook" model is the most powerful growth strategy in financial directories
- Credit Karma proved that a return-visit habit (checking your score monthly) creates a captive audience
- CreditDoc's equivalent: a "Credit Health Dashboard" that combines CFPB complaint data, credit score simulation, and personalized lender matching
- The approval odds concept (personalized matching) is why Credit Karma earns premium commissions vs. generic affiliate links
- Capital efficiency: Credit Karma needed only ~$5M to build the business

### 1.3 LendingTree — $1.12B Revenue (2025)

**How they started:**
Founded 1996 by Doug Lebda with $30,000 as "CreditSource USA." Lebda's idea: what if borrowers could get competing loan offers from multiple lenders? This was radical in 1996 — previously you walked into ONE bank and took whatever rate they gave you. Launched nationwide in 1998 as the first online loan marketplace. IPO in February 2000, weeks before the dot-com crash.

**Revenue model — the lead-gen machine:**
1. **Consumer fills out a form** with financial situation, property info, desired loan terms
2. **Platform matches** the request against 500+ lender partners
3. **Consumer receives 3-5 competing offers** from different lenders
4. **Lenders pay LendingTree** for the introduction:
   - Cost-per-lead basis: $30-$100 per lead (non-exclusive)
   - Cost-per-click basis on rate comparison tables
   - Percentage of closed loans for some programs
5. **Insurance segment** (now largest): $711.9M in 2025 (Progressive alone = 27% of total revenue)

**Time to first revenue:**
- 1996-1998: Building platform, pre-revenue
- 1998: First revenue from lender lead fees at nationwide launch
- 2000: IPO — revenue was real but modest
- 2003: Acquired by IAC (Barry Diller), forced to prove profitability
- 2025: $1.12B revenue, insurance segment dominance

**SEO strategy:**
- Content marketing through CompareCards (acquired) for credit card SEO
- Programmatic pages for every loan type + location combination
- MagnifyMoney acquisition (2017) added editorial content authority
- Rate comparison tables earn organic traffic for "best mortgage rates" queries

**Key inflection point:**
The pivot to insurance. Insurance segment grew from a small add-on to $711.9M in 2025 (120% growth in 2024). Insurance lead gen is structurally more profitable than mortgage leads — higher volume, less rate-sensitive, more carrier partners. Carriers ranked 4-10 grew revenue 65% YoY, showing diversification beyond Progressive.

**Data moat:**
- 500+ financial partner network — lenders stay because consumers are there, consumers stay because lenders compete
- Historical lead conversion data allows pricing optimization
- Cross-sell engine: someone looking for a mortgage also needs insurance, credit cards, personal loans
- Brand: "When banks compete, you win" is one of the most recognized financial taglines

**Current traffic:** Not publicly disclosed separately; significant organic + paid

**What CreditDoc can learn:**
- The marketplace model (consumers fill out forms, companies pay for leads) is more valuable long-term than affiliate links
- CreditDoc's "What's My Borrowing Power?" quiz is a proto-lead-gen form — capture user info, match to lenders
- Insurance was LendingTree's surprise growth driver — CreditDoc should think about adjacent verticals
- The key insight: owning the lead funnel is more valuable than owning the content. Content drives traffic, but the form captures value.
- LendingTree's non-exclusive leads at $30-100 each are achievable at much lower traffic levels than affiliate commissions

### 1.4 Bankrate — Acquired for $1.24B by Red Ventures

**How they started:**
Founded 1976 by Robert K. Heady as "Bank Rate Monitor" — a print newsletter tracking bank interest rates. Went online in 1996. IPO in 1999, raised $45.5M. Spent 20+ years building the definitive rate comparison platform.

**Revenue model:**
- Display advertising (historically primary)
- Affiliate commissions on financial product recommendations
- Licensed rate data to media outlets and financial institutions
- Sponsored content / native advertising

**Time to first revenue:**
Revenue from day one — it was a paid print newsletter starting in 1976. Online revenue began in 1996. By acquisition, it generated 8.4M organic sessions/month with $30.6M traffic value.

**SEO strategy — the "$76M SEO Moat":**
1. **Pillar page architecture:** The mortgage page alone drives 4.4M organic sessions/month ($20M+ traffic value). Each pillar links to blog posts, tools, and comparison guides that link back.
2. **Editorial firewall:** Explicit separation between editorial and advertising. Writers reference government agencies and academic research. Fact-checkers review everything. Gold standard for YMYL E-E-A-T.
3. **Annual survey data:** Financial Security Index survey generates hundreds of backlinks from top-tier publications every year.
4. **Content freshness:** Rates change constantly; Bankrate updates obsessively. This freshness signal is a ranking advantage.
5. **Certified experts:** Chief Financial Analyst Greg McBride (CFA) — real credentials that differentiate content.

**Key inflection point:**
The 1996 move from print to online. Bankrate had 20 years of financial data authority from its print newsletter. When it went online, that authority transferred to its domain. Most new financial directories don't have this luxury — they start from zero authority.

**Data moat:**
- 40+ years of financial rate data history
- Editorial reputation built over decades
- E-E-A-T signals that new competitors can't easily replicate
- Part of Red Ventures portfolio (also owns CNET, The Points Guy, Healthline)

**What CreditDoc can learn:**
- The editorial firewall is non-negotiable for YMYL credibility. CreditDoc's disclosure/methodology pages are a good start.
- Bankrate's pillar page strategy is the template: one pillar page per major category, 20-30 spoke pages linked to it
- Annual data reports (Financial Security Index equivalent) are link magnets that compound over years
- CreditDoc's "State of Credit Repair in America" report using CFPB data is the direct equivalent
- The Red Ventures acquisition shows that financial directories with strong SEO moats have enormous exit value

### 1.8 The Points Guy (TPG) — $50M+ Revenue, Acquired by Red Ventures

**How they started:**
Brian Kelly launched it as a personal blog in 2010 while working at Morgan Stanley. Started monetizing via affiliate marketing in February 2011. Within six months, blog traffic exploded. Within two years, making serious money — banks started calling him to collaborate.

**Revenue model:**
- Credit card affiliate commissions (virtually 100% of revenue)
- Single credit card signup = up to $120 commission. High-volume publishers can earn 4x that ($480/signup)
- 2019: $50M revenue, profitable every year of existence
- 2018: revenue grew 50%+ YoY

**Time to first revenue:**
- 2010: Blog launched, no revenue
- February 2011: First affiliate monetization
- 2011-2012: Revenue exploding, quit Morgan Stanley
- 2019: $50M revenue, 100 employees, 8M monthly uniques

**SEO strategy:**
- 44% of traffic from organic search
- 15-person SEO team in Charlotte + 15-person tech team in Austin
- Acquired competitors: Mommy Points, Travel is Free, Million Mile Secrets (consolidation play)
- Grew 28% by expanding content from credit card obsessives to novices

**Key inflection point:**
Negotiating exclusive commission rates above market standard. When you drive enough volume, credit card companies pay premium rates. Kelly went from standard $50-100/signup to $120+ (up to $480 for high-volume). Volume + exclusivity = exponential revenue.

**What CreditDoc can learn:**
- A single-person blog in financial services can reach $50M revenue through affiliates
- Negotiating exclusive/premium affiliate rates is a multiplier — once CreditDoc has volume
- Acquiring smaller competitors is a growth strategy: buy sites with rankings in your niche
- The travel/points niche is comparable to credit repair: high-value affiliate commissions, passionate audience

## 2. NON-FINANCIAL DIRECTORY DEEP DIVES {#2-non-financial-directories}

### 2.1 Care.com — Acquired by IAC for $500M

**How they started:**
Founded 2006 by Sheila Lirio Marcelo. Started as a childcare marketplace, then expanded to elder care, pet care, tutoring, housekeeping. By 2012: 7M users across 15 countries. By Q3 2019: 374,000 paying families, 1.5M successful matches since inception.

**Revenue model — the subscription marketplace:**
1. **Family subscriptions (primary):** Free basic membership to view profiles; premium membership ($37/month, less for quarterly/annual) to contact caregivers, access background checks, and use advanced search
2. **Caregiver subscriptions:** Premium for better visibility and job access
3. **Background check fees:** $9-$59 per check
4. **Placement fees:** One-time fee for specialized placements (nanny, senior care)
5. **Corporate B2B (Care@Work):** Companies pay to offer caregiving benefits to employees — subscription or pay-per-use
6. **Advertising:** Targeted ads to their user base

**Growth strategy:**
- International expansion through acquisitions (Betreut in Germany, launched in UK/Canada)
- Vertical expansion beyond childcare: senior care, pet care, tutoring, housekeeping
- Corporate partnerships as B2B revenue stream
- 2018: 3 acquisitions (Town + Country, Galore/Care.com Explore, Trusted)

**Key inflection point:**
The launch of Care@Work — selling caregiver benefits to corporations as an employee perk. This B2B channel provided predictable recurring revenue at higher margins than consumer subscriptions.

**What CreditDoc can learn:**
- Care.com's subscription model (free to browse, pay to contact) maps to CreditDoc's premium listings concept: free lender profiles, lenders pay to claim/enhance
- The B2B play (selling to credit repair companies, financial advisors, credit unions) is more predictable than consumer affiliate revenue
- Vertical expansion works: Care.com went from childcare to 5+ verticals. CreditDoc can expand from credit repair to insurance, tax help, bankruptcy attorneys
- $500M acquisition on ~$200M revenue shows 2.5x revenue multiple for care marketplace

### 2.2 Zillow — $2.6B Revenue (2025), 221M Monthly Users

**How they started:**
Founded 2004 by Rich Barton and Lloyd Frink (ex-Microsoft, Expedia founders). Raised $6M Series A, $25M Series B (Benchmark). Launched February 2006 with the Zestimate — 1 million visitors in the first 2 days. Critical detail: Zillow had NO real estate listings for the first 3 years. They only had Zestimates.

**Revenue model:**
- Premier Agent program (65%+ of revenue): agents pay for visibility on listings and buyer leads (pay-per-lead)
- Residential: agent and software offerings, New Construction marketplace ($418M Q4 2025)
- Mortgages: loan origination ($57M Q4 2025, +39% YoY)
- Rentals: rental advertising
- 2025: $2.6B total revenue, +16% YoY

**SEO strategy — programmatic at maximum scale:**
- 5.2M+ pages, virtually all programmatically created
- Non-programmatic pages (blog, careers) = less than 1,000
- 33M+ organic visits/month according to Ahrefs
- Each property page is unique (unique data: Zestimate, tax history, sales history, school ratings, neighborhood data)
- 127M average monthly unique visitors (Comscore Q4 2025)

**Key inflection point:**
The Zestimate. "Come for the tool. Stay for the inventory." Zillow attracted massive demand with a free tool before they even had listings. The tool was novel, fun, voyeuristic ("what's my house worth?"), and required zero supply-side inventory. For 3 years, the tool WAS the product.

**Data moat:**
- Zestimate algorithm trained on 110M+ homes
- Property history, tax records, school data, neighborhood statistics
- User behavior data from 221M monthly users
- XML syndication protocol (2007) created a standard that agents adopted, creating network effects

**What CreditDoc can learn:**
- **"Come for the tool. Stay for the inventory."** This is the single most important lesson. Build a free tool so compelling that people visit CreditDoc before they even care about the directory. The "What's My Borrowing Power?" quiz or a Credit Score Simulator is CreditDoc's Zestimate.
- Zillow proved that programmatic SEO works at massive scale WHEN each page has genuinely unique data. CreditDoc's 30K profiles need genuinely unique data per page (CFPB complaints, regulatory status, specific services, local context).
- No listings for 3 years. Zillow didn't wait for supply to build demand. CreditDoc doesn't need active lender participation to build traffic — the data IS the product.
- 221M monthly users on $2.6B revenue = ~$12 revenue per user/year. CreditDoc at 100K monthly users with similar ARPU math: $1.2M/year potential.

### 2.3 Avvo — 100M+ Annual Visits, Acquired by Internet Brands

**How they started:**
Founded 2006 by Mark Britton (former Expedia legal counsel). Rich Barton (Expedia/Zillow founder) was a key advisor. Initial funding: $13M from Benchmark Capital and Ignition Partners. Total raised: $132M.

**Revenue model:**
- Premium attorney profiles (primary): lawyers paid to claim/enhance profiles
- Advertising on attorney search pages
- Avvo Legal Services: fixed-fee legal services marketplace
- Q&A forum drove traffic but wasn't directly monetized

**SEO strategy — the pre-population playbook:**
1. **Scraped public bar association data** to create profiles for 97% of all licensed attorneys in the US
2. **Lawyers couldn't ignore it** because their profile already existed — with or without their participation
3. **Avvo Rating algorithm:** Proprietary rating based on experience, reviews, peer endorsements, disciplinary history. Controversial but drove consumer trust.
4. **Q&A forum:** 150,000+ lawyers answered legal questions for free to build profile reputation. Created massive long-tail UGC content.
5. **Result:** 100M+ annual visits, became the de facto lawyer directory

**Key inflection point:**
The pre-population decision. By scraping public data and creating profiles for every licensed attorney, Avvo solved the cold-start problem instantly. Lawyers came to claim their profiles (because the profiles existed whether they liked it or not), which drove engagement, which drove traffic, which attracted more lawyers.

**Data moat:**
- 97% coverage of US attorneys
- Disciplinary history from bar associations (public data others didn't organize)
- 150K+ lawyer answers in Q&A forum (UGC moat)
- Avvo Rating as proprietary metric

**Why they sold instead of IPO'd:**
Mark Britton explicitly cited Google as the reason: "Google has become much more aggressive when it comes to local search." Google's own business listings, Maps, and later AI Overviews threatened Avvo's organic traffic. Scale through consolidation (Internet Brands owned Lawyers.com, Martindale-Hubbell, Nolo) was the defensive play.

**What CreditDoc can learn:**
- CreditDoc IS doing the Avvo playbook: pre-populated profiles from public data (FDIC, NCUA, HUD, CFPB). This is exactly right.
- The "claim your profile" premium model comes AFTER traffic. Avvo built audience first, then monetized with premium profiles.
- The Google risk is real and current. Avvo sold because Google ate their lunch on local search. CreditDoc needs brand, email, and direct traffic — not just SEO.
- Q&A/community features create massive UGC content for long-tail SEO (future CreditDoc play)

### 2.6 Yelp — $1.46B Revenue (2025), 265M+ Reviews

**How they started:**
Founded October 2004 by Jeremy Stoppelman and Russ Simmons (ex-PayPal engineers). The initial idea: an email-based referral network. Stoppelman had the flu and couldn't find a doctor recommendation online. Built a site where you email friends asking for local business recommendations.

**First review:** October 12, 2004. But the launch flopped — few users beyond friends and family.

**The pivot that saved Yelp:**
In February 2005, co-founder asked: "Should there be a way to write a review without being asked a question?" This changed everything. The site relaunched letting anyone write reviews unprompted.

**The Yelp Elite Squad:**
Stoppelman hired a "buzz-marketing guru" who selected the most active reviewers and threw them an open-bar party, calling them the "Yelp Elite Squad." ## 5. HOW CREDIT KARMA MONETIZES WITHOUT CHARGING USERS {#5-credit-karma-monetization}

### The Full Revenue Stack

**Layer 1 — The Free Hook:**
Free credit scores and credit monitoring. Before Credit Karma, this cost $15-20/month from the credit bureaus. Making it free attracted 110M+ members.

**Layer 2 — The Data Layer:**
Every member provides their full credit profile. Credit Karma sees: credit score, outstanding debts, payment history, credit age, inquiries, derogatory marks. This data is the fuel for everything else.

**Layer 3 — The Matching Engine:**
Credit Karma's AI runs 60B+ daily predictions. It compares your credit profile against historical approval data: "People with your profile who applied for [Card X] were approved 87% of the time." This "Approval Odds" feature converts at dramatically higher rates than generic affiliate links because users trust the personalized recommendation.

**Layer 4 — The Commission:**
When a matched user applies for and is approved for a credit card, loan, or insurance product, the financial institution pays Credit Karma a commission. Because Credit Karma pre-qualifies users (showing only products they're likely to be approved for), conversion rates are higher, and Credit Karma commands premium commissions.

**Layer 5 — Ancillary Revenue:**
- Interchange fees from Credit Karma debit card (1.4-2.5% of each transaction)
- Interest income on user deposits
- Tax filing services (post-Intuit acquisition)

### Why This Model Works

The key insight: **Credit Karma makes money when users make good financial decisions.** If a user applies for a card they won't be approved for, Credit Karma earns nothing. So the incentives are aligned — better recommendations = more approvals = more revenue.

This is fundamentally different from generic affiliate sites that earn a commission per click regardless of outcome. Credit Karma earns per approval, which means they're motivated to match accurately.

### 2025 Financial Performance Under Intuit

- Credit Karma revenue: $2.3B (grew 32% YoY)
- Q4 2025: $649M (grew 34% YoY)
- Now combined with TurboTax into Intuit's "Consumer" segment
- Cross-selling between tax filing and credit products drives additional revenue

### What CreditDoc Can Replicate

CreditDoc can't replicate the credit score data layer. But it CAN replicate the matching concept:
- User answers 5-question quiz (credit score range, goal, urgency, location, income range)
- CreditDoc matches them to relevant lenders from its 28K+ database

## 7. HOW PROGRAMMATIC SEO DIRECTORIES AVOID THIN CONTENT PENALTIES {#7-programmatic-seo}

### The 2025-2026 Reality

Google's March 2026 core update explicitly named "scaled content abuse" as a violation. Sites generating thousands of near-identical pages through AI or template automation without genuine added value saw ranking losses of 60-90% almost overnight.

### What Gets Penalized

1. **Template pages with city name swapped:** "Credit repair in [Houston/Dallas/Austin]" where only the city name changes
2. **Less than 300 unique words per page:** Below this threshold, Google's quality classifiers flag it
3. **Poor engagement signals:** Users immediately leaving, bouncing to Google, or using site search instead of reading
4. **No entity differentiation:** Pages about different businesses that contain identical structure and template language

### What Works (Proven by Zillow, Yelp, TripAdvisor)

1. **Real data differentiation per page:** Each Zillow property page has unique data (Zestimate, tax history, sales history, school ratings). Each Yelp business page has unique reviews, photos, hours, menu items. Target: 30-40% content differentiation between pages.

2. **500+ unique words per page:** This is the safe floor. Under 500 unique words risks classification as thin content.

3. **Entity authority signals:** Google weights content differentiation 3-4x more than technical signals in post-update ranking recovery. Fixing Core Web Vitals on thin content does NOT produce recovery — content must be fixed first.

4. **Strategic noindexing:** Pages that aren't ready yet should be noindexed. Only index pages with complete, unique content. Zillow indexes 5.2M pages but they ALL have unique data.

5. **Engagement proof:** Internal linking that keeps users exploring. Related content recommendations. Interactive elements that increase time on page.

### CreditDoc-Specific Guidance

CreditDoc's 28K+ profiles need to pass these tests:
- Does each profile have 500+ unique words? (CFPB data, regulatory info, diagnosis, services, reviews)
- Does each profile differ by 30-40% from similar profiles? (Different complaint data, different services, different location context)
- Do users engage with the page? (Interact with maps, click related lenders, read complaint data)
- Are skeleton pages noindexed? (Current policy is correct — maintain it aggressively)

The enrichment pipeline (50/day converting skeleton to quality) is the right approach. Don't index a page until it has genuine unique value.
