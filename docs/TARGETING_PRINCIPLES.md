# Keyword targeting principles — what we learned working through this

**Last updated:** 2026-04-26
**Why this exists:** so we never have to redo this conversation. These are the rules that distinguish *real* keyword discovery from template-driven generic seeds. Apply them every time a new pillar / money page / intent gets defined.

---

## RULE -3 — NO PAID ADS, EVER

> "We cannot run paid ads. I have always lost money with that model." — Gian, Apr 26

CreditDoc.co is built as an organic-traffic business. **No Google Ads, no Facebook Ads, no display, no retargeting, no paid acquisition of any kind.** Ever. Not at $0/mo revenue, not at $50K/mo, not at $500K/mo.

Why this is binding (not just preferred):
- **Unit economics:** affiliate finance routing through 2-3 intermediaries means CAC > LTV at any meaningful paid scale. Industry-wide, affiliate finance sites lose money on paid acquisition >90% of the time.
- **Network discounts:** affiliate networks (CJ, Impact, Awin, Bankrate Card Network) discount or reject leads from paid traffic — degrading the per-lead value and risking account termination.
- **Brand competition:** lenders bid against you on their own brand keywords; your CPC is structurally higher than theirs.
- **Evaporation:** organic SEO compounds. Paid traffic stops the moment you stop paying.
- **Strategic moat:** organic ranking is defensible (takes years to build, harder to copy). Paid traffic is rented and identical to every competitor's.
- **Founder history:** Gian has personally lost money on this model previously. Ruled out from his own evidence.

What this means:
- All traffic acquisition tactics in the plan are organic: SEO, content, internal linking, HARO/PR, partnerships, social seeding.
- Distribution accelerators we use: Reddit/Quora seeding (tasteful), HARO responses, broken-link outreach, partnerships with adjacent finance creators, podcast appearances, owned email list.
- We don't budget for paid traffic, period. The reinvestment trigger ($50K/mo) does NOT include "ramp paid ads."

If a future agent or contractor proposes paid ads as a growth lever — even a small test — the answer is no. Re-read this rule. The trade-off has already been weighed by the founder against direct prior experience.

## RULE -2 — DO NOT GET FLAGGED FOR SCALED AI CONTENT ABUSE

> "We need to be careful not to get flagged for automatic content. I am worried it can destroy us if Google decides we are pushing out too much AI content." — Gian, Apr 26

Google's **March 2024 spam update** introduced explicit policy on "scaled content abuse" — mass-produced AI/programmatic content with insufficient editorial value. Enforcement has been brutal: sites that pushed 1,000+ AI pages in short windows have been algorithmically deranked or fully deindexed. This is the **single biggest existential risk** to the directory.

**For a 41-day-old domain, the safe-harbor profile is:**
- Slow, deliberate publishing pace (1–3 deeply-built pages per weekday max)
- Each page has either (a) genuine editorial value or (b) is anchored on real first-party data
- Templated programmatic content (geo pages, lender profiles) is safer than templated opinion content (`/answers/` answers)
- Editorial review on a sample of every batch
- Avoid the "spray 10K geo pages at once" pattern even if our pipeline could produce them

**Concrete rules for our pipeline:**

1. `/answers/` page pace: stay at **1 weekday, max 3 weekday** until we have at least 60 pages indexed and ranking. Then re-evaluate. **Do not bulk-generate.**

2. Geo layer rollout is **waved**, not big-bang:
   - Wave 1: 50–100 highest-priority city pages, ship over 4–6 weeks. Validate indexation + initial ranking.
   - Wave 2: scale to 500 if Wave 1 is healthy. Spread over 8–12 weeks.
   - Wave 3 onward: only if Wave 1 + 2 are ranking and not penalized.

3. Each programmatic page (geo or money sub-page) **must consume real first-party data** — actual lender count, real Google Places info, real state regulator data. Pure-template content gets flagged. The directory's existing Google Places enrichment is what makes this safe.

4. Each `/answers/` page generated by Claude CLI **must pass the existing 65-point compliance gate** AND get sample editorial review (at minimum 1-in-10 manually checked by Gian or designated reviewer).

5. **No duplicate templates across geo pages.** Each page has unique data injected. If a city has only 1 lender for that pillar, we don't write the page — thin content fails.

6. **No mass-generation events.** If the executor cron lags, do NOT catch up by publishing 20 pages in one day. Catch up at 1.5x normal rate maximum.

7. **Voice variation** in `/answers/` content. Identical voice across 200 pages reads like a single AI prompt. Vary tone slightly across personas / page types.

**The strategy implication:** the framework still works, but the Year-2 numbers come slower. Better to hit $80K/mo by month 18 with the directory intact than chase $80K/mo by month 12 and lose everything to a manual penalty.

**The rule:** when in doubt, publish less. Authority compounds faster on a 60-page-old, well-built directory than a 600-page-old penalized one.

## RULE -1 — IT'S ALL LENDING. THE NICHE FRAMING IS A RED HERRING.

> "Farming is also on my list of expansions for the rest of the year. People will say it's not a niche but it's lending." — Gian, Apr 26

The conventional SEO advice is "pick a niche." For a lending directory, that advice is wrong. **The niche IS lending.** Every vertical we add — farming loans, equipment financing, auto refi, mortgage adjacencies, student loan refi, RV/boat loans, healthcare practice loans, cannabis lending — is the same underlying intent: someone needs capital, finds CreditDoc, gets routed to the right product.

This means:
- We don't apologize for breadth across lending verticals.
- Adding agricultural/farming/equipment as new intent families isn't "diluting focus" — it's **filling out the surface of the same business**.
- The framework's 7 pillars are the real boundary. Within each pillar we cover the full lending universe relevant to that pillar.
- Adjacent lending types we don't currently cover (auto, mortgage, home equity, student refi, boat/RV) are roadmap candidates, NOT distractions.

**The rule:** when evaluating whether to add a new lending angle, the test is "does this map to one of the 7 pillars?" not "is this a niche?" If it maps, we cover it.

## RULE 0 — TARGETING JUST THE HEAD TERM IS SUICIDE

> "Targeting just the term itself we are cooked." — Gian, Apr 26

The directory cannot win on "best small business loans," "best personal loans," "best credit cards." Those are NerdWallet / Bankrate / SBA.gov / Wells Fargo territory. DR 80–95+. A 41-day-old site never reaches them.

**The only winnable game is the wide net of conversational long-tail and qualification-anxiety questions.** Every single one of the rules below is in service of this rule. If a proposed keyword is a head term and nothing else, we don't target it. Head terms get tracked (because they're what we eventually want to rank for as authority compounds), but the *content production effort* always goes toward the long-tail constellation.

---

## 1. The gold isn't head terms — it's qualification-anxiety questions

Generic head terms ("best small business loans") are NerdWallet/Bankrate territory. Unwinnable for a young directory. **The real targets are the questions someone asks when they have a problem that maps to one of our products** — the question reveals their need before they know the product name.

Examples (Pillar 1):
- "is it possible to take a loan after 6 months of opening your business" → P1 best-startup-business-loans
- "can a business that is new take a loan" → P1 best-startup-business-loans
- "do I need to be profitable to get a loan for my business" → P1 best-startup-business-loans

Cross-pillar example: "will a charge-off stop my driver's license application" looks like a regulatory question, but the searcher has a credit problem → P6 credit-repair lead.

**The rule:** for every intent we define, brainstorm the questions a real person would ask in their *own* words about the *underlying problem*, not about the *product they should buy*.

---

## 2. Vary the phrasing — real people don't talk like keyword tools

Templates produce robot-sounding seeds ("loan for business 6 months in business"). Real people type:
- "business loans with only 6 months"
- "business loans without track record"
- "what interest rate will a small business get"
- "how can a new business get a loan"
- "what's the best way for a small business to get a loan"

Same intent. Five different phrasings. Each one has its own autocomplete tree, its own PAA box, its own competitor SERP. We need to seed all five.

**The rule:** for every intent, write 25–40 natural-language paraphrases. Mix question-form, statement-form, and synonym-replaced variants. Don't stop at the obvious phrasing — three lateral phrasings that all map to the same intent are worth more than the head term.

---

## 3. Synonym layers multiply the surface

Same intent, different vocabulary:
- "small business loans" = "small business borrowing" = "small business funding" = "small business financing" = "small business capital" = "money for a small business"
- "bad credit" = "poor credit" = "low credit score" = "damaged credit" = "subprime credit" = "credit problems"
- "new business" = "startup" = "brand new business" = "recently opened" = "just started"
- "qualify" = "approve" = "get accepted" = "be eligible" = "get approved for"

Each synonym swap creates a new searcher segment — different people use different vocabularies for the same underlying problem.

**The rule:** for each intent, draft a synonym matrix and apply at least 2–3 vocabulary variations to the most important phrasings.

---

## 4. Use the questions to generate the ideas

Don't start with "what keywords should we target?" Start with "what questions does our ideal customer ask?" The questions ARE the keyword research, because:
- Questions reveal the searcher's mental state before they know the answer
- Questions have lower competition than commercial terms
- Questions are how Google's PAA boxes are structured — ranking on a question gets you a SERP feature

**The rule:** when expanding any intent, the first 10 phrases we write should be questions, not commercial phrases.

---

## 5. Lateral angles — every term has 8 angles

For any commercial root, real searchers come at it from 8 distinct angles. Generic seeds miss most of them:

| Angle | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Direct variants** | "X", "best X", "top X", "X 2026" |
| **Qualification anxiety** | "what credit score for X", "can I get X with bad credit" |
| **Process / mechanics** | "how does X work", "documents needed for X", "how long does X take" |
| **Comparison** | "X vs Y", "is X worth it", "X alternatives" |
| **Objection / concern** | "why did my X get denied", "are X hard to get", "X pros and cons" |
| **Outcome / consequence** | "what if I default on X", "X tax deduction", "does X affect credit" |
| **Research / data** | "X reddit", "average X amount", "typical X interest rate" |
| **Lateral context** | "X vs savings", "X during recession", "X for first time" |

**The rule:** every money page must be expanded across all 8 angles. Codified in `keyword_engine/stages/lateral_expander.py` — 50 templates per page.

---

## 6. The alphabet trick — Google reveals lateral topics

Querying autocomplete for "<topic> a", "<topic> b", ... "<topic> z" surfaces hundreds of lateral topics that weren't in our original seed list. From "small business loans a..z" we found:
- States: Arizona, Alabama, California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Detroit, Dallas, El Paso, ...
- Banks: Bank of America, BofA, BMO, Chase, Capital One, Citizens
- Niches: EIN-only loans, no credit check, instant approval, easy approval, easy to get
- Events: during covid, during pandemic, big beautiful bill (OBBBA), disaster relief
- Audiences: disabled veterans (subgroup of veterans), felons (extreme niche), women start-up

These don't come from templates — they come from Google itself. Every priority money page should get an alphabet-trick discovery pass.

**The rule:** every priority money page gets a `python explore_page.py <slug>` deep-discovery pass. Output goes to `data/intermediate/explore_<slug>.md` for human scanning.

---

## 7. Personas / narrative case-studies layer on top

Stories anchor multiple anxieties to one character → ranks for long-tail story queries AND wires 4-5 money pages together via in-context internal links. Frey Chu's topical-authority flywheel.

Example persona (from seo_web.yaml):
- Bob Carter, 8-month-old restaurant in Buffalo, bad personal credit from 2019 medical bankruptcy, two teenage daughters
- Story touches: P1 startup-business-loans (his loan need), P1 bad-credit-business-loans (his credit overlay), P6 credit-repair-after-bankruptcy (fixing his score), P5 monitoring (tracking recovery), P2 personal loans (cash crunch)
- Story page ranks for: "restaurant business loan after bankruptcy", "Buffalo NY restaurant funding", "8 month old restaurant loan options"

**The rule:** every priority money page should get at least one persona-driven case study. Personas live in `seo_web.yaml` under `personas:`.

---

## 8. Filter foreign-market questions early — don't waste budget

Autocomplete will surface "small business loans Bahamas / Dubai / Edmonton / BC / Barbados / Canada / UK / Australia". The directory is US-only. These get sidelined to `intl_questions.csv` in Stage 2. Don't burn KE / DataForSEO credits scoring them. Don't write content for them.

---

## 9. Save everything as we go

Manual paraphrase work + per-page deep-discovery output is hours of effort. Lose it once and we redo. Persistent storage:
- `seo_web.yaml` — the intent + paraphrase library
- `data/intermediate/explore_<slug>.md` — per-page deep discovery
- `_project/MONEY_PAGES_INVENTORY.md` — the 26 money pages catalog
- `_project/PIPELINE_LOGIC.md` — the 5-stage pipeline doc
- `_project/SESSION_HANDOFF.md` — for the next Claude session

## How a new pillar / money page / intent gets defined

When adding to the SEO web:

1. **Define the intent** in `seo_web.yaml` — description, primary_money_page, secondary_money_pages
2. **Write 25–40 natural-language paraphrases** — questions first, then statements, then synonym-swapped variants
3. **Run `python explore_page.py <slug>`** for the primary money page if it's a hub
4. **Add a persona** if the intent is high-priority (Pillar 1, 2, 3 hubs especially)
5. **Update this principles doc** if a new pattern emerges that should become a rule
